# NetStim — warm, figure-led website direction

Design brief, 11 September 2026. This is a proposed layout and art direction, not a rendered mock or a visual audit. Browser capture of the named references is currently blocked. Arc's appearance has not been verified in this turn; the starting point is the user's preference for Arc, with more warmth and Lead-DBS imagery.

## The direction

An editorial research website that feels like a place where scientists work: warm paper backgrounds, confident dark typography, generous space, spectacular scientific figures, and real people. The science should carry the visual identity. Keep the most saturated colors inside Lead-DBS figures, where color has meaning.

Use soft ivory (#F6F3EB), charcoal (#242723), muted moss (#556747), and a restrained warm rust (#A65037) for interface accents. These are proposed design colors, not established NetStim brand tokens. Use a restrained serif for major headings and a clear sans serif for navigation, body copy, and figure captions; test Source Serif 4 with Source Sans 3. Avoid applying editorial recoloring to scientific overlays or tractography legends.

## Homepage composition

Desktop target: 1440 px wide; approximately 1160 px content width, with airy margins. Mobile becomes a single readable column with the title before the scientific figure.

1. **Header.** NetStim wordmark at left. Research, People, Tools & Data, Publications, and Join Us at right. Institute affiliation appears as a quiet line, not a row of badges. Use the actual logo when obtained; do not invent a replacement.
2. **Hero.** A narrow text column takes about two-fifths of the width; a large, unboxed Lead-DBS brain render takes the rest. Proposed heading: “Understanding and modulating brain networks.” Supporting copy places the institute at University Hospital Cologne and states its neurological and psychiatric research aim. One primary link: “Explore our research.” A quieter link leads to Lead-DBS. The figure has a small real caption and credit.
3. **Research, through figures.** One broad editorial spread with a full scientific image and a short explanation of the question it addresses. Use the existing deep brain stimulation topic first. No invented paper title, discovery, outcome, or figure caption. Source the figure from an approved lab asset before filling the spread.
4. **Tools & data.** Two spacious rows: Lead-DBS, then normative connectomes. Each pairs a real figure thumbnail with a concise introduction and its existing destination. Distinguish software from downloadable datasets through labels and links, rather than ornamental cards.
5. **Publications.** A restrained heading and link to the current publication page. Add three named papers only after bibliography verification. Do not label older selected papers “Latest.”
6. **People and conversations.** A real team or journal-club photograph gives the page its human warmth. Bring Stimulating Brains and Cologne's Stimu-Latte into a small editorial section with clear destinations where available. The existing content supports these entries; do not invent a meeting date or signup route.
7. **Footer.** Institute identity and contact route, funding, imprint, privacy, and social links. Keep historical funding available without allowing a long sponsor list to dominate the page.

The current content allocation is grounded in the [NetStim homepage](https://www.netstim.org/), read on 11 September 2026. The wording above is a design proposal, not approved replacement copy. The parallel content audit owns factual updates.

## Blender / Lead-DBS hero specification

Make one beautiful scientific scene, not a generic glowing “AI brain.” Start from a lab-approved Lead-DBS scene or export: actual cortical geometry, selected subcortical structures, a defensible electrode reconstruction, and relevant pathways. Preserve anatomical relationships and the meaning of all overlays. Do not manufacture patient geometry or imply that an artistic model demonstrates a result.

Composition: three-quarter brain view on transparent or warm ivory background; the focal anatomy occupies the right side, leaving quiet space toward the title. Use an understated translucent cortex, clear internal structures, soft studio illumination, and delicate contact shadows. Figure colors should remain faithful to their source. Any editorial illustration must be explicitly captioned as illustrative.

The first deliverable should be a high-quality still with a wide desktop crop and a separately composed mobile crop. A later 6–8 second, slow camera movement could reveal depth without moving anatomical structures relative to each other. Render a lightweight video with a static poster and reduced-motion fallback. Avoid a live 3D viewer for the initial homepage: the research question and figure should remain immediately readable. Provide pause control if motion loops; keep scientific captions static and accessible.

Asset checklist: source scene or geometry; approved viewpoint; anatomical/overlay provenance; permission and credit; scientist-approved caption; desktop and mobile stills. These are production inputs, not an assertion that existing assets have been found or licensed.

There is a historical precedent for the pipeline: Andreas Horn describes exporting electrode `.ply` files for Blender in the official [Lead-DBS support forum](https://www.lead-dbs.org/forums/topic/speeding-up-leaddbs/). That establishes a plausible route, not a verified workflow for the current installed Lead-DBS version or a complete brain-scene export.
