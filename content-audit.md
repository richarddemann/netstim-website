# NetStim: proposed content refresh

Reviewed 11 September 2026. Editorial draft for review; nothing has been changed on the live website. Public pages can lag internal decisions. “Verified” below means supported by the cited public source, not independently confirmed by the institute.

## 1. Homepage: make the current identity immediate

**Current excerpt:** “DEAR READER”. The introduction already locates the institute in Cologne. This is a presentation problem rather than an outdated location. [Current page](https://www.netstim.org/)

**Proposed replacement**

> Understanding brain networks. Improving brain stimulation.
>
> We are the Institute for Network Stimulation at University Hospital Cologne. We study how brain networks contribute to neurological and psychiatric symptoms, and how stimulation can help us understand and modulate these networks.
>
> Our work brings together neuroimaging, computational methods and neuromodulation research.
>
> Explore our research · Meet the team

**Confidence:** High for identity and research scope. The headline describes an aim, not a claim of proven clinical benefit. Supported by the [current university institute page](https://netstim.uni-koeln.de/) and [Cologne appointment announcement](https://www.uk-koeln.de/uniklinik-koeln/aktuelles/detailansicht/neuer-stiftungsprofessor-fuer-computationale-neurologie/).

**Software section replacement**

> Open tools for brain stimulation research
>
> Together with collaborators around the world, we develop Lead-DBS: open-source software for reconstructing DBS electrodes and simulating stimulation from postoperative MRI and CT images.
>
> Explore Lead-DBS

**Confidence:** High. Preserve the shared authorship of the project. [Lead-DBS](https://www.lead-dbs.org/)

**Other homepage edits:** Replace the journal-name parade with selected papers, each showing an actual title, year, short research question and figure. This is editorial selection, not an assertion that the existing list is wrong. Keep Stimu-Latte and Stimulating Brains prominent: they supply precisely the human warmth the redesign needs. Remove the duplicate German Research Council entry in past funding. Do not infer new grant status from individual staff funding. [Current homepage](https://www.netstim.org/)

Keep the previous training in an expandable full biography. Keep a short personal paragraph about music, coffee or podcasts; ask Andreas which anecdote still fits. Do not present former Berlin PhD-program affiliations as ended solely because he moved.

## 3. Jobs: stop making Berlin the default route

**Current excerpt:** “Currently no open posts”. The following section routes prospective applicants toward Berlin PhD programs. This may remain relevant for some collaborations, but does not explain how to join the Cologne institute. [Jobs](https://www.netstim.org/about-us/jobs/)

**Proposed replacement**

> Join NetStim
>
> Interested in brain networks, neuromodulation or open scientific software? We welcome enquiries from people interested in working with our team in Cologne, including those exploring fellowship-supported projects.
>
> Tell us about your research interests, relevant experience and the kind of project you would like to pursue.
>
> Our work combines neuroimaging and brain stimulation research with contributions to Lead-DBS, including software development, documentation, testing and teaching.
>
> Contact the team

**Confidence:** Medium. Welcoming unsolicited enquiries is already stated. The proposed requested information and contact workflow are editorial suggestions. The lab must confirm the receiving address, capacity to host projects and current vacancies before publishing. Do not automatically replace Berlin programs with a Cologne program: eligibility and supervision arrangements require confirmation.

The existing no-vacancy statement can remain only after a current staff check, with a visible review date. Use a clear culture paragraph instead of the unexplained “teal organization” label; preserve the lab manual link after checking its destination. [Jobs](https://www.netstim.org/about-us/jobs/)

## 5. DBS research: replace dated impact framing

**Current excerpts:** “downloaded over 65,000 times”; “cognitive disorders such as Depression”. The impact section is dominated by 2015–2017 milestones and an undated usage count. The clinical classification is also an awkward umbrella. [DBS page](https://www.netstim.org/research-interests/deep-brain-stimulation/)

**Proposed introduction**

> Deep brain stimulation and brain networks
>
> We investigate how the location of stimulation and its connections to the wider brain relate to clinical outcomes. Our research combines electrode reconstruction, neuroimaging and computational modelling to study the networks involved in neurological and psychiatric symptoms.

**Confidence:** High for broad research scope. [University institute page](https://netstim.uni-koeln.de/)

**Proposed impact-section replacement**

> Built with the research community
>
> Lead-DBS brings electrode reconstruction, stimulation modelling and anatomical resources into a shared research workflow. NetStim contributes to its development alongside an international community of collaborators.
>
> Software and documentation · Contributors · Methods papers

**Confidence:** High for general capabilities and collaborative development. [Lead-DBS](https://www.lead-dbs.org/)

Move historic awards into a dated history section. Remove the download count unless the team can provide a dated, defined measure. Keep the 2023 release story as history and link to the software’s current release page rather than claiming it is the latest. Retain methodological papers but organize by the research task they support. [DBS page](https://www.netstim.org/research-interests/deep-brain-stimulation/)

## 6. Naming, links and contact information

The page titles still identify a laboratory while the homepage introduces an institute. Adopt **NetStim — Institute for Network Stimulation** as the proposed public masthead, with **University Hospital Cologne** as the location line; retain “lab” naturally in informal copy. The formal institute name is supported by the [university site](https://netstim.uni-koeln.de/). Branding capitalization remains a team decision.

Several internal destinations still use netstim.berlin. Do not blindly replace the domain: check each equivalent page and preserve working redirects. This review establishes legacy destinations, not that they are broken. Check the imprint and privacy pages with their responsible owner before migrating contact details.
