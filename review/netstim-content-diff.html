07 · DBS · Introduction

Current text

The main focus of our work is deep brain stimulation (DBS) – predominantly in movement disorders such as Parkinson’s Disease, Essential Tremor or Dystonia – more rarely in cognitive disorders such as Depression or Alzheimer’s Disease. In DBS, electrodes are surgically implanted into deep (subcortical) areas of the brain and – using an implanted impulse generator – these structures are stimulated chronically. The figure below shows a reconstruction of two DBS leads implanted with target subthalamic nucleus (orange) to treat Parkinson’s Disease.

Proposed text

We investigate how the location of deep brain stimulation and its connections to other brain regions relate to clinical outcomes. We reconstruct electrode locations from postoperative imaging and use structural and functional connectivity to study the networks associated with improvement in symptoms. Our work also asks how stimulation of a small brain region affects activity across a wider network. In DBS, electrodes are surgically implanted into deep (subcortical) areas of the brain and – using an implanted impulse generator – these structures are stimulated chronically. The figure below shows a reconstruction of two DBS leads implanted with target subthalamic nucleus (orange) to treat Parkinson’s Disease.
