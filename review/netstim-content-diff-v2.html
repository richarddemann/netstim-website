Coverage and limitations

Reviewed the main public content pages: Home, People (all visible profiles and alumni names), Andreas’s separate page, Jobs, DBS, Normative Connectomes, Publications, Posters, Book, Videos & Talks, Stimu-Latte, Updates (first page), public onboarding page and Privacy. Also compared the local mock. This is a page-level editorial review, not an exhaustive crawl of every archived post, scientific reference, download or embedded media item. The imprint, Internal Links and Events could not be retrieved; the external manual and dynamic bibliography remain unverified. Web results have crawl dates ranging from today to three months ago, so time-sensitive items require a direct owner check.

Writing references and what changed

Revision 2: removed the slogans, restored scientific detail and kept edits small where the original works. Reference pages inform the writing and structure, not claims about NetStim.

  • NIMLab / Center for Brain Circuit Therapeutics — Explains the scientific approach in steps and separates it from institutional history. Useful for research structure. The lab identifies its home as Brigham and Women’s, within Mass General Brigham; it should not be labelled an MGH lab.
  • UCSF Starr Lab — Research — Names the questions, then describes how the group investigates them. This is the strongest model for the NetStim research page.
  • UCSF N3 Lab — Names methods and research aims, with uncertainty about what the work may lead to. Publications are identified as papers or preprints.
  • Fixel — de Hemptinne Laboratory — Moves from the research aim to the recordings used to investigate it. Use that specificity; the page’s older news is not a model for currency.
  • Fixel-linked Butson Lab — A short description of the work followed by Projects, Publications and Resources. Brevity works because the verbs describe research activities.
  • UCSF Starr Lab — People — Names and current roles are easy to find. NetStim can do this without removing its longer personal biographies.
  • Mass General Brigham — Center for Brain Circuit Therapeutics — A patient-facing page organised around conditions, care and appointments. Useful for distinguishing audiences; its care claims do not belong in NetStim research copy.
  • Fixel Institute — An institute homepage serving patients, donors and researchers. Its promotional headline is a less suitable writing reference for NetStim than its individual lab descriptions.

Institute for Network Stimulation We study how brain networks respond to stimulation and how these responses relate to neurological and psychiatric symptoms. Our group is based at University Hospital Cologne. We use structural and functional MRI, diffusion imaging and computational modelling to investigate these questions. Use the institute name as the heading. Describe the research and methods underneath. The original welcome could also stay; a new slogan is unnecessary. Scope and methods come from the NetStim pages reviewed, not from the reference labs.
